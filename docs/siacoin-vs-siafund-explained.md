# Siacoin vs. Siafunds: What SF Is and Why Your Wallet Shows Both

New Sia Wallet users occasionally notice a second, unfamiliar balance line — Siafunds (SF) — sitting next to their Siacoin (SC) balance, usually at zero, and reasonably wonder what it is and whether they're supposed to do anything with it.

## Two separate assets, one wallet

Siacoin (SC) is the network's utility token — the currency that pays hosts for storage and collateralizes storage contracts. It has an inflationary, proof-of-work-driven supply and is what people mean by default when they say "Sia."

Siafunds (SF) are a completely separate asset with a fixed, tiny supply: a governance-and-revenue-share token that entitles holders to a percentage of the fees generated by storage contracts across the network. Where Siacoin functions like the network's operating currency, Siafunds function more like an equity stake in the protocol's transaction-fee revenue. The two live on the same blockchain and are both supported natively by any wallet built for Sia — including Sia Wallet — but they behave completely differently as assets.

## Why your balance shows SF at zero

Most holders never acquire Siafunds at all — they aren't distributed the way Siacoin is, and there's no mining or faucet mechanism for them. If your wallet shows an SF balance of zero, that's simply the normal state for a wallet that has only ever held Siacoin. It's not a bug, and it doesn't mean anything is missing from your setup.

## If you migrated from Sia-UI and see SF

Anyone migrating a legacy Sia-UI seed through Sia Wallet's built-in migration assistant will have both their Siacoin and any Siafund balance derived and moved together automatically — the migration flow handles SF exactly the same way it handles SC, deriving legacy addresses for both and consolidating them into your new wallet in the same transaction. If you happened to acquire Siafunds at some point in Sia's history, they'll show up correctly after migration without any separate steps.

## Sending, receiving, and holding SF

Functionally, sending and receiving Siafunds in Sia Wallet works the same as Siacoin — same address format, same transaction flow, same wallet UI. The practical difference is entirely in what each asset represents and how liquid it is: SF has a far smaller circulating supply and much thinner trading markets than SC, so if you do hold Siafunds, treat it as a long-term, illiquid position rather than something to actively trade day to day.

## The comparison in one line

If Siacoin is the currency that flows through the Sia network paying for storage, Siafunds is closer to a stake in the toll booth — a claim on a slice of the fees that currency generates as it moves. Most Siacoin holders will never need to think about Siafunds at all; the wallet supports both mainly so that the small number of people who do hold SF aren't left with a wallet that can't see it.
